Self-Reflection: Networking Strategies & Mastering the Interview
As a 26-year-old international student now pursuing the Job Search and Success course after three years as a C++ and real-time OS developer at Tencent, this term has been a deeply introspective period of growth. Transitioning from a fast-paced engineering role into a new professional ecosystem has been both exciting and humbling. During the second half of the course, two modules — Networking Strategies and Mastering the Interview — reshaped not just how I see job hunting, but how I view myself in this new phase of my career.

Networking Strategies
What:
Before coming to Canada, networking was a term I mostly associated with corporate events or social obligations. During my time at Tencent, career progression was typically driven by performance metrics and seniority rather than proactive networking. The module on Networking Strategies changed my perspective. I learned that over 85% of jobs are filled through networking​
— a statistic that stunned me and made me rethink my passive approach.

One activity that stood out was the exercise where we crafted a personalized LinkedIn message, similar to the example of Sam connecting with the Lego Brand Manager. I used this format to message a former Tencent colleague now working at NVIDIA in Canada. I personalized it by referencing our shared project on the low-latency messaging system we built, and asked if he’d be open to a quick coffee chat. To my surprise, he replied the same day and we scheduled a Zoom call.

So What:
This experience proved that even a single thoughtful message could reignite professional relationships and open doors. It also reminded me that my past work still holds value here. Previously, I underestimated how transferable my experience was outside China — I feared being seen only as a "coder" with little soft skill. But reconnecting with my network and learning to present myself authentically helped me feel less like an outsider.

Now What:
I've committed to engaging more regularly with my network — commenting on posts, attending Lambton-hosted tech meetups, and following up with professionals I meet. I’ve already expanded my LinkedIn by 40 quality connections over 6 weeks. I'm also planning to conduct an in-person informational interview with an AI infrastructure lead I met through a mutual connection. The long-term goal? To create an intentional network that supports my transition into roles blending systems engineering and AI product development.

Mastering the Interview
What:
The Master the Interview module was perhaps the most transformational. Interviews at Tencent were highly technical — think debugging sessions and code performance analysis. Behavioral interviews? Rare. Here, however, they are the standard, and mastering them requires storytelling, reflection, and emotional intelligence — areas I hadn't practiced enough.

The VMock interview activity gave me an eye-opening look at my current readiness. My first score was 57%. The feedback was clear: I needed better posture, stronger speech delivery, and more vivid STAR examples. For instance, when asked about a time I demonstrated leadership, my answer lacked emotional depth. After reviewing examples and practicing, my final attempt scored 83% — a clear sign of growth.

So What:
I realized that effective interviewing is not just about listing achievements, but about making a human connection and showing how I think. One of my refined STAR responses centered around when I led a transition from a legacy build system to Bazel at Tencent. I structured it to highlight how I aligned cross-functional teams, handled resistance from senior engineers, and delivered performance gains — all in a compelling narrative that made my technical story accessible.

Now What:
I plan to continue practicing interview responses with peers and leveraging AI tools like VMock. I’ve bookmarked several common behavioral questions and tailored my responses to blend both technical and personal elements. I also took the initiative to schedule a mock interview with my Co-op Advisor. Preparing questions for employers — like how their team fosters continuous learning or manages cross-regional collaboration — is something I now include in every prep session.

Conclusion
This second half of the course has done more than equip me with techniques — it has reframed how I view my own potential. I came in hesitant about how my past fit into the North American job market. I’m leaving this term with not only clarity, but confidence. I now see networking not as transactional, but as relationship-building. I view interviews not as tests, but as conversations to express impact.

Career Alignment:
These reflections directly align with my goal to work in a technical program management or AI infrastructure role in Canada. I’m now crafting my resume and elevator pitch to reflect not just my coding experience, but also my leadership, adaptability, and international experience. I’ve also added key skills like communication and cross-cultural collaboration — things I now recognize employers deeply value.
